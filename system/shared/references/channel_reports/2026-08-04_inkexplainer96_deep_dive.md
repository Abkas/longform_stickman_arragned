## Narration style — the core of what to replicate

Pulled full transcripts (auto-captions) for two videos: the top hit (**Rain**, 1.07M views) and the weakest recent one (**Guns**, 16K views) — saved to `system/shared/references/transcripts/`. Style is highly consistent across both despite the performance gap, which means this is a deliberate house style, not something that varies video-to-video.

**1. Cold open — either immersive second-person or a present-tense shock stat**
- Rain: *"Imagine you haven't eaten in 2 days. You know exactly where the herd is. You tracked it yesterday... And then it starts raining."*
- Guns: *"Somewhere on Earth in the next 2 minutes, a human being is going to be shot. That's the average and it holds every single day."*
Both cold opens avoid any channel intro/branding — straight into the scenario within the first sentence.

**2. Escalating "stakes list," almost always framed as a countable set**
- Rain: *"that same rain... could kill you in three different ways before it even stopped. The first one is cold... The second one is flooding... And the third one, maybe the worst, is predators."*
- This rule-of-three (or similar small-N list) structure recurs constantly — it's used for stakes, for fire-kit contents, for social payoff items, etc. It gives the middle of the video a scannable skeleton.

**3. Recurring signpost phrases that re-hook mid-video** (not just at the cold open):
- "Here's why that was so terrifying." / "Here's where it gets really interesting." / "Here's the accident inside the accident." / "But here's the thing." / "Here's the part nobody talks about."
These function as mini-cliffhangers planted every 60-90 seconds to prevent drop-off through the middle of a long video — worth treating as a reusable phrase bank.

**4. Register shifts between second-person immersion and third-person factual delivery**
The cold open and occasional re-engagement beats ("Now put yourself on that wall...") are second-person/present-tense. The evidence-heavy middle sections shift to plain third-person historical narration ("Controlled fire evidence goes back at least 400,000 years..."). This alternation is what keeps a fact-dense script from feeling like a lecture.

**5. Sentence rhythm — short declaratives mixed with one longer explanatory sentence, and frequent sentence-initial conjunctions**
Lots of sentences start with "So," "But," "And," "Because" — this reads as spoken/conversational rather than written-essay prose, and deliberately breaks "proper" grammar rules for pacing. Short sentences land the punchline; a longer sentence right after unpacks it.

**6. Every claim is anchored to a specific name + number, never a vague generality**
"Qesem Cave, Israel," "5,300-year-old," "Polly Wiessner's 2014 study," "80% of the conversation" — this is the mechanism behind the "research-backed" brand promise, and it's consistent sentence-by-sentence, not just a citation list at the end.

**7. Closing callback that loops back to the cold open, restating the thesis with a twist**
- Rain opens on "you're trapped," closes on "you get creative, and that might be the single biggest reason we're still here" — reframing the opening crisis as the origin of the payoff.
- Guns opens "somewhere on Earth in the next 2 minutes, a human being is going to be shot," and closes by literally repeating that exact line — a hard callback, not just a thematic echo.

**8. A consistent narrative thesis: "nobody planned this" / accidental causality**
Both scripts frame their entire subject as an unintended consequence chain: rain-forced downtime "accidentally" seeded human culture; alchemists searching for immortality "accidentally" invented weapons. This isn't just a hook gimmick — it's the throughline that gives a fact-dump a single argument to hang on. Worth deciding a version of this for our own scripts (the existing formula in `system/03_script/structure.md`'s "reframe/upside twist" step is this same device).

**9. Mid-roll sponsor placement pattern (Guns video only — the channel does take sponsors)**
The hook question ("So how does that happen?") is deliberately left unresolved, then immediately cut to a ~90-second sponsor read (World of Warships), then resolved right after ("All right, back to the question that started all of this."). Classic hook-then-monetize-then-resolve structure — relevant once our own channel is far enough along to take sponsors.

**10. Sourcing format in the video description** (from the Rain video, verbatim style):
```
▸ Qesem Cave, Israel — "Evidence for the repeated use of a central hearth at Middle
Pleistocene (300 ky ago) Qesem Cave, Israel" (2014, Journal of Archaeological Science)
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2013.11.015
```
Every major claim gets a `▸` bullet with a short label, the paper's actual title in quotes, (year, journal), and a DOI link where available. Tags are minimal (4-5 broad tags like "stone age," "ancient history"), category is "People & Blogs."

## What to concretely replicate

1. **Cold open in the first sentence** — no channel intro, straight into either a second-person scenario or a present-tense shock stat.
2. **Build a phrase bank** from the signposting list above ("here's the thing," "here's where it gets interesting," etc.) and plant one every ~60-90 seconds through the middle section.
3. **Rule-of-three stakes/lists** wherever you're listing dangers, evidence, or outcomes — don't just narrate a flat sequence.
4. **Alternate register**: second-person for hooks/re-engagement beats, third-person factual for evidence delivery.
5. **Every fact needs a name + number** — no unattributed generalities in the script.
6. **End on a hard callback** to the exact cold-open line or image, not just a generic summary.
7. **Design one recurring "host" stick character** for brand recognition across thumbnails/videos, the way Ink Explainer's messy-brown-hair character does.
8. **Match the sourcing format** in descriptions (`▸` + title-in-quotes + year/journal + DOI) — it's cheap to do and is core to the credibility brand.
9. **Target cadence**: roughly one video every 9-10 days once past the launch phase; runtime settling around 11-13 min.
10. **Stay anchored to "ancient/prehistoric human" topics specifically** — general-psychology tangents are this channel's least reliable category, both on this channel and presumably for a clone of it.
