## What the data actually says

1. **Explicit "Ancient/Early Humans" anchor correlates hard with performance.** 6 of the top 7 videos name it directly in the title. The two clearest misses on this axis — IKEA Effect and Life Speeds Up — are also the two lowest-performing titles that aren't the immature "Guns" upload. This isn't optional; it's the single strongest signal in the data.
2. **Stakes level matters more than topic novelty.** "Rained All Week" (sustained crisis) and "Only Human Species Left" (existential) crush "got Sick" (individual, mundane) even though all three have a human-history anchor. A forced-constraint scenario needs to threaten survival or feel species-scale — not just describe an inconvenience.
3. **"Origin of a very normal modern habit" is a reliable sub-pattern.** Alcohol, Weed, Clothes all land mid-to-hit purely on the curiosity gap of "when did humans start doing this thing everyone does today." Worth keeping as a fallback pattern independent of the crisis-scenario formula.
4. **Question-form titles cluster at the top; statement-form ones cluster mid-tier.** 4 of the top 4 are questions. Statement-form only works when paired with a strong evocative adjective ("Real Reason," "Disturbing Ways") — a flat statement with no adjective and no anchor (IKEA Effect) is the single worst performer in the catalog.
5. **Length sits in a narrow band: 7-9 words.** Outside that band doesn't obviously help or hurt on its own, but it's worth using as a default target since it's what the whole hit cluster shares.

## Working title formula

`[How/What/When/Why] Did/Do Ancient Humans [verb phrase describing a forced, sustained, survival-level constraint]?`

— with `The [strong adjective] [noun/way] Ancient Humans [verb phrase]` as the fallback statement-form pattern when a question phrasing feels clunky.

Non-negotiable per the data above: **the title must name "Ancient/Early Humans" explicitly, and the scenario must read as a sustained or existential threat, not a mundane occurrence.** Target 7-9 words.
