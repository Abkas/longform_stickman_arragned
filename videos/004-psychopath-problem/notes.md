## Real sources (verified, all real published work)

1. **Linda Mealey, 1995, "The Sociobiology of Sociopathy: An Integrated
   Evolutionary Model"** (*Behavioral and Brain Sciences*) — the founding
   frequency-dependent-selection hypothesis: psychopathy/sociopathy isn't a
   malfunction, it's a stable alternative life-history strategy that only
   remains viable while rare (a "cheater" strategy exploiting a cooperating
   population). Splits real-world outcome into "primary" (more
   temperamental/heritable) vs. "secondary" (more environmentally-driven)
   sociopathy — script simplifies this to "successful" vs. "unsuccessful"
   (caught vs. never caught) for narrative clarity, a distinction Mealey's
   own paper and later literature both use.
2. **Axelrod's iterated Prisoner's Dilemma tournaments (Robert Axelrod,
   late 1970s/1980s)** — real computer-tournament research showing
   tit-for-tat (cooperate first, then mirror the opponent's last move) beats
   both always-defect and always-cooperate strategies over repeated rounds.
   Used as the game-theory backbone for why a rare-cheater equilibrium is
   mathematically stable, not just a metaphor.
3. **Side-blotched lizard (*Uta stansburiana*) rock-paper-scissors mating
   system** — real, published herpetology finding (first described by
   Barry Sinervo & Curt Lively, 1996, *Nature*): orange-throat, blue-throat,
   yellow-throat males run three distinct, cyclically-dominant mating
   strategies (aggressive territory-holder, cooperative territory-holder,
   sneaker), frequency-dependent in exactly the same mathematical shape as
   Mealey's human hypothesis. Real, verifiable, not an invented analogy.
4. **MAOA gene / "warrior gene" — Caspi et al., 2002, *Science*,
   "Role of Genotype in the Cycle of Violence in Maltreated Children"** —
   the real, careful, replicated finding: a low-activity MAOA variant
   predicted later antisocial/violent behavior *only* in boys who had also
   experienced serious childhood maltreatment; the same variant with a
   stable non-abusive childhood showed no elevated risk. This is the
   correct, non-oversold version of the finding — script must preserve the
   gene-by-environment interaction, never claim a standalone "psychopath
   gene."
5. **Psychopathy prevalence estimates** — real, disputed range depending on
   measurement instrument: roughly 1% of the general population using the
   strict Hare Psychopathy Checklist (PCL-R) cutoff, climbing toward 4-5%
   using looser/broader criteria in some studies. Present as a genuine
   disputed range, not a single fabricated precise number.
6. **Cheater-detection cognition — Cosmides & Tooby's Wason selection task
   research (Leda Cosmides & John Tooby, UC Santa Barbara, multiple papers
   from the late 1980s/1990s)** — real, replicated finding: abstract logic
   puzzles are solved poorly by most people, but the identical logical
   structure reframed as "detect someone breaking a social exchange/deal"
   is solved dramatically better, cross-culturally, including in
   non-Western hunter-gatherer populations with no exposure to formal
   logic puzzles. Real academic debate exists over whether this reflects a
   dedicated "cheater-detection module" specifically vs. a broader
   relevance/familiarity effect — script should note the debate exists
   rather than oversell "a cheater-detection organ."
7. **Corporate psychopathy prevalence — Robert Hare & Paul Babiak,
   *Snakes in Suits* (2006) and related published research** — real
   finding: using strict clinical criteria, corporate/executive
   populations show psychopathic-trait prevalence estimated at roughly
   3x the general-population rate. Hare is the actual author of the
   PCL-R, the clinical gold-standard checklist; Babiak is an
   organizational psychologist. Their B-Scan 360 is a real, published
   corporate screening instrument built specifically to catch these
   traits in a hiring/promotion context.
