## Topic

~74,000 years ago, the Toba supervolcano erupts in Sumatra — the largest eruption in the last 2 million years, ash reaching sites 9,000 km away in South Africa. Popular narrative: it nearly wiped out humanity (the "volcanic winter bottleneck" theory). Real evidence (Smith et al. 2018, *Nature* — Pinnacle Point & Vleesbaai, South Africa): Toba ash is embedded directly inside an occupation layer, and human activity (tools, fires, bones) *increased* after the eruption, not stopped.

Contrarian myth-busting hook, and it lines up naturally with the channel's "reframe" structural beat: crisis assumption -> real evidence -> humans didn't just survive, they thrived.
